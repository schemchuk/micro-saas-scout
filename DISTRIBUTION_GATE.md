# Distribution Evidence Gate

- **Date:** 2026-09-22

Research agents were instructed and confirmed to operate read-only (no file writes, no git operations); all synthesis and the gate decision below were performed by the orchestrating pass, not by the research agents.

- **Gates tested:** Distribution Evidence (`BASELINE.md` §9) only — Path A (observed newcomer acquisition) and Path B (structurally newcomer-friendly channel), per-channel, per-candidate.

## Candidate 1 — Insurance Commission Reconciliation

### Buyer
Independent US P&C/multi-line insurance agencies — agency principals, operations/bookkeeping staff, commission/accounting specialists.

### Candidate channels investigated

#### Channel: Independent agent associations (Big "I" / IIABA)
- Buyer presence: yes (this is the buyer's own trade association).
- Entrant access: no public vendor directory open to unknown software vendors was found; the association's "member benefits"/"Belong" pages reference negotiated group discounts with pre-selected large vendors (e.g. DocuSign, UPS), not a self-serve listing.
- Discovery mechanism: unconfirmed — no evidence of an open vendor directory generating inquiries.
- Recent newcomer evidence: none found.
- Structural restrictions: appears to require a pre-existing vendor relationship/negotiation, not open participation.
- Source/date: independentagent.com (primary, page undated, accessed 2026-09-22).
- Evidence strength: **Weak / no evidence found.**

#### Channel: AMS marketplace — Applied Epic Vendor Certification Program
- Buyer presence: yes — Applied Epic is a widely used AMS among the target agencies.
- Entrant access: a formal Vendor Certification Program launched August 2026 (within the freshness window); application via form/email, no stated cost, no explicit written requirement for an existing customer base.
- Discovery mechanism: certified vendors get listed on Applied's public "Certified Vendors" ecosystem page — a real, live, non-paid discovery surface.
- Recent newcomer evidence: the initial certified cohort (Ascend, Adapt, Coverflow, Fulcrum, Inteveo EcoLink, Lightspeed Voice, Outmarket, Quandri, RecordLinker, Sonant, Xilo) consists entirely of already-funded, already-staffed insurtechs with existing agency customers (e.g. Xilo: founded 2018, "implemented into hundreds of agencies") — **no solo-founder/pre-traction entrant was found in the cohort**, suggesting an informal traction bar even though none is written down.
- Structural restrictions: none explicitly stated, but cohort composition is a real negative signal for Path B criteria (4) ranking-without-traction and (6) realistic unknown-entrant path — both remain **unverified**, not confirmed.
- Source/date: globenewswire.com press release, 2026-08-12 (primary); www1.appliedsystems.com Certified Vendors page (primary), both accessed 2026-09-22.
- Evidence strength: **Path B, partially verified — the strongest single lead for this candidate, but two of six required criteria are unverified/negative-leaning. Medium, not strong.**

#### Channel: HawkSoft AMS marketplace
- Buyer presence: yes, for agencies on HawkSoft.
- Entrant access: two tiers (API Partners, Solution Partners); HawkSoft's own 2023 post describes "exploring minimum thresholds of known mutual customers" and negotiating terms per partner — a curated, relationship-based process, not self-serve listing.
- Discovery mechanism: no "new/recently added" surfacing feature found.
- Recent newcomer evidence: none found.
- Structural restrictions: appears to require HawkSoft's own vetting/relationship — the opposite of newcomer-friendly.
- Source/date: blog.hawksoft.com, dated 2023 (primary, but **stale — outside the 12-month freshness window**), accessed 2026-09-22.
- Evidence strength: **Weak-to-unverified Path B; stale evidence.**

#### Channel: AMS user conferences (Applied Net / Applied Client Network)
- Buyer presence: yes.
- Entrant access: exhibitor booth starts at **$5,500** for a 10x10 space, plus conference registration.
- Discovery mechanism: paid sponsorship/exhibit hall.
- Recent newcomer evidence: not applicable — this is a paid channel by design.
- Structural restrictions: explicitly pay-to-play.
- Source/date: acnsponsorships.org, appliednet.com/pricing (primary), accessed 2026-09-22.
- Evidence strength: **Not evidence for this gate's purpose** — structurally the opposite of a no-cold-outreach, organic discovery channel.

#### Channel: FMO/IMO buyer's guides
- Buyer presence: partially — FMOs/IMOs serve a related but not identical buyer segment.
- Entrant access: several "commission software buyer's guide" pages exist, but they are vendor/SEO content published by commission-software companies or affiliate sites, not independent FMO editorial content.
- Discovery mechanism: unconfirmed — no evidence a small vendor received real leads.
- Recent newcomer evidence: none found.
- Source/date: enrollhere.com, fullcast.com, corecommissions.com (self-interested vendor content), accessed 2026-09-22.
- Evidence strength: **Weak / no evidence found**, and the sources themselves are vendor self-report, not independent.

#### Channel: Insurance industry publications (Insurance Journal, Insurance Business America)
- Buyer presence: yes, these are widely read by agency principals.
- Entrant access: unconfirmed — no organic editorial coverage of a small commission-reconciliation vendor was located; search results were general insurtech trend pieces unrelated to this category.
- Recent newcomer evidence: none found.
- Source/date: insurancejournal.com/insurtech (primary), accessed 2026-09-22.
- Evidence strength: **Weak / no evidence found.**

#### Channel: Catalyit Solution Provider Directory
- Buyer presence: yes, targets independent agencies specifically.
- Entrant access: shows "New in 2025" listings, but appears to require a "Partner with Catalyit" onboarding, likely paid/tiered.
- Discovery mechanism: searchable catalog; no data found on whether listings drive real inquiries.
- Recent newcomer evidence: none confirmed (listing existence only).
- Source/date: catalyit.com, accessed 2026-09-22.
- Evidence strength: **Weak — directory existence only, not confirmed as generating acquisition.**

### Strongest verified distribution path
Applied Epic's Vendor Certification Program (launched August 2026) is the strongest lead: it is live, free to apply to, and produces a real public discovery surface. However, it does not reach a fully verified Path B — the composition of its own first cohort (all already-funded, already-traction-bearing vendors) is a direct, evidence-based reason to doubt that a true unknown/solo-founder entrant would be surfaced the same way. No Path A (an actual observed case of a small/unknown vendor acquiring buyers through any investigated channel) was found for this candidate.

### Distribution assessment
Every other channel investigated (associations, FMO/IMO guides, industry press, HawkSoft marketplace, AMS conferences, Catalyit) returned either no evidence, stale evidence, or evidence that the channel is paid/relationship-gated rather than organically newcomer-friendly. The one channel that is structurally promising (Applied Epic certification) has two of six required Path B criteria unverified, with the available evidence (cohort composition) leaning negative on both. This does not meet the bar for a demonstrated distribution path, but nothing found rises to a proven structural impossibility either — several channels remain genuinely under-researched (comparison/review-site ranking mechanics for zero-review new entrants were not investigated in depth).

### Gate result
**BLOCKED / INCONCLUSIVE**

### Remaining unknowns
Whether a genuinely unknown/solo-founder vendor (not an already-funded insurtech) could successfully complete Applied's Vendor Certification Program and be meaningfully surfaced by it. Whether Big "I"/IIABA operates any open vendor-directory mechanism not found in this pass. Whether comparison/review sites (G2, Capterra, GetApp) meaningfully surface a zero-review new entrant to this buyer — not investigated in depth.

## Candidate 2 — Multichannel E-Commerce Accounting Reconciliation

### Buyer
Small-to-mid multichannel e-commerce sellers (Amazon/Shopify/eBay/Etsy/Walmart) and the bookkeepers/accountants who manage their books on Xero/QuickBooks Online.

**Note:** Link My Books' acquisition by Visma (January 2025) is explicitly excluded from this analysis as distribution evidence, per task instruction — it is a fact about corporate ownership, not about newcomer discoverability, and is not cited below in support of any conclusion.

### Candidate channels investigated

#### Channel: Shopify App Store
- Buyer presence: confirmed — merchants search the App Store directly (secondary estimate: ~60% of installs originate from search, unverifiable against a primary Shopify figure).
- Entrant access: self-serve listing is open to any developer.
- Discovery mechanism: Shopify's own documentation (shopify.dev, primary) states "Built for Shopify apps appear higher in Shopify App Store search rankings," with badge eligibility requiring "quality through installs, reviews, and ratings over time" — an **explicit, official, install/review-gated ranking boost**. Independent ASO-agency analysis (secondary, multiple sources, 2025–2026) converges on install velocity, review velocity, and listing freshness dominating actual search ranking.
- Recent newcomer evidence: one Shopify Community forum self-report ("Marius_S," dated 2026, secondary/unverified) of 9 organic installs in two weeks from a keyword-tagline change — real and dated, but a single unverified anecdote, not in the accounting/reconciliation category, and modest in scale.
- Structural restrictions: the accounting/reconciliation category itself already has apps with 16–217 reviews (Dext Commerce, Synder, MyWorks Sync, Reconcilely, Bookkeep — confirmed live listings, apps.shopify.com, accessed 2026-09-22), against which a zero-review entrant is structurally disadvantaged per Shopify's own stated ranking factors.
- Source/date: shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/built-for-shopify (primary, accessed 2026-09-22); community.shopify.com thread (secondary, accessed 2026-09-22).
- Evidence strength: **Fails Path B criterion (4) explicitly, by Shopify's own documentation. One thin, unverified Path-A-shaped anecdote outside the relevant category. Weak-to-medium, not sufficient alone.**

#### Channel: QuickBooks App Store
- Buyer presence: unconfirmed — no evidence found that e-commerce bookkeepers actually browse this marketplace specifically to source reconciliation tools.
- Entrant access: Intuit's own developer blog runs a recurring "newest apps" feature (primary, blogs.intuit.com, dated May 2025 and Aug 2024) — a structural placement for new entrants regardless of install count.
- Discovery mechanism: confirmed feature exists; buyer usage of it for this specific purpose unconfirmed.
- Recent newcomer evidence: none found for a reconciliation-category vendor specifically.
- Structural restrictions: none found preventing listing.
- Source/date: blogs.intuit.com (primary), accessed 2026-09-22.
- Evidence strength: **Weak / lead-only** — satisfies some Path B mechanics but not buyer-usage confirmation.

#### Channel: Xero App Marketplace
- Buyer presence: yes, plausible for Xero-using sellers/bookkeepers.
- Entrant access: **confirmed negative finding** — Xero's own certification-checkpoints documentation (developer.xero.com, primary, current) states: "Effective March 2026, we have shifted to a tiered pricing model based on connections and API usage. To be listed on the Xero App Store, you will need to be in the Plus tier or higher" — a **confirmed paid gate on listing itself**.
- Discovery mechanism: Xero also promotes a "Most recommended by advisors" surfacing mechanism (secondary, blog.xero.com) that privileges apps already recommended by accountants — a popularity-based mechanism, not newcomer-friendly.
- Recent newcomer evidence: none found.
- Structural restrictions: confirmed paid tier requirement (real, primary-sourced, current as of March 2026).
- Source/date: developer.xero.com/documentation/xero-app-store/app-partner-guides/certification-checkpoints (primary, accessed 2026-09-22).
- Evidence strength: **Weak, with a confirmed structural negative finding (paid listing gate).**

#### Channel: Amazon Selling Partner Appstore
- Buyer presence: yes, for Amazon-seller-facing tools.
- Entrant access: listing requires developer registration, an assessment, and Amazon approval (3–4 weeks; "all software partners are vetted") — a vetting gate, not explicitly a large-customer-base gate.
- Discovery mechanism: keyword/category/curated collections, plus filters by "reviews" and "trending/recommended" — surfacing leans on review/usage signals once live.
- Recent newcomer evidence: none found for a small Amazon-seller accounting/reconciliation tool.
- Source/date: developer-docs.amazon.com (primary), accessed 2026-09-22.
- Evidence strength: **Weak / lead-only.**

#### Channel: E-commerce bookkeeper/accountant communities (Facebook groups etc.)
- Buyer presence: unconfirmed for e-commerce-specific focus (generic bookkeeper groups found, not confirmed as multichannel-e-commerce-specific).
- Entrant access: unconfirmed self-promotion policy for any specific, named, currently-active group.
- Discovery mechanism: unconfirmed.
- Recent newcomer evidence: none found.
- Source/date: general search, accessed 2026-09-22.
- Evidence strength: **Weak — exactly the "there's a Facebook group" pattern the standard excludes; no group-specific verification completed.**

### Strongest verified distribution path
No channel investigated reaches a fully verified Path A or Path B. The Shopify App Store is the closest to a partial Path B — genuine buyer presence and self-serve listing access are real — but Shopify's own documentation confirms ranking is install/review-weighted, which directly fails Path B's criterion (4), and the one available newcomer-installs anecdote is outside the relevant category and unverified independently.

### Distribution assessment
This candidate shows the most consistently negative findings of the three: two channels (Shopify, Xero) produced **confirmed, primary-sourced structural findings against newcomer discoverability** (review/install-weighted ranking; a paid listing-tier requirement), rather than merely absent evidence. QuickBooks, Amazon, and bookkeeper communities remain unverified leads. Per the gate's own guardrails, this is not itself grounds for KILL — "a marketplace is competitive" and "incumbents have many reviews" are explicitly listed as insufficient KILL reasons — but it means no credible channel was demonstrated among those investigated, and some genuinely relevant channels (accountant conferences, YouTube/podcast sponsorships, other e-commerce seller communities) were not investigated in this pass and remain open.

### Gate result
**BLOCKED / INCONCLUSIVE**

### Remaining unknowns
Whether e-commerce bookkeepers meaningfully use the QuickBooks App Store's "newest apps" feature to actually source tools. Whether any specific, named, currently-active bookkeeper/accountant community permits organic vendor discovery. Whether accountant-conference or educational-content channels (not investigated this pass) offer a viable path. Whether a zero-review Amazon Appstore listing gets meaningful browse traffic in practice.

## Candidate 3 — eBay/Etsy Cross-Listing & Listing-Template Management

### Buyer
eBay/Etsy sellers, multichannel resellers, power sellers displaced by InkFrog's April–June 2026 shutdown.

### Candidate channels investigated

#### Channel: eBay Community seller forums
- Buyer presence: confirmed — this is where prior Switching Feasibility research already found named users (andy.johns_4, lit1213) discovering LitCommerce and FolderLister.
- Entrant access: eBay's own Community Content Policy (ebay.com, primary, accessed 2026-09-22) explicitly **bans** "promoting or advertising to buy, sell, or trade any product or service listed or located outside of any eBay property" — a vendor cannot self-promote. Discovery can only happen through third-party peer recommendation (another seller naming a tool in response to a question), not vendor-initiated posting.
- Discovery mechanism: peer Q&A threads ("what tool should I use").
- Recent newcomer evidence / repeatability — **the key finding for this candidate**: a thread ("What's a good eBay seller software to use?," community.ebay.com, original post ~2023) is **still receiving organic replies as recently as ~mid-September 2026** — i.e., this Q&A pattern is a standing, recurring community behavior, not an artifact of the InkFrog shutdown specifically. However, the tools named in that particular (non-shutdown) thread are all established, multi-year vendors (3Dsellers, Auto Reprice io, GarageSale, Sixbit, InkFrog) — **no small/new vendor was found being organically surfaced in a non-shutdown-linked instance**. The strongest evidence of an actual small vendor (LitCommerce, FolderLister) being discovered through this channel remains specifically tied to the InkFrog-shutdown window (~May 2026).
- Structural restrictions: self-promotion ban (above); otherwise open to any member.
- Source/date: ebay.com/help/policies (primary, undated, accessed 2026-09-22); community.ebay.com threads (secondary/user-generated, dated ~2023 with replies through ~Sept 2026), accessed 2026-09-22.
- Evidence strength: **Path A, partially confirmed.** The channel/mechanism is demonstrably repeatable (confirmed via activity independent of the shutdown event), but the only confirmed small-vendor-discovery instance available is itself shutdown-linked — this is exactly the "temporary migration opportunity vs. repeatable channel" distinction the task asked to resolve, and it resolves as **not fully resolved**: the mechanism recurs, but a small-vendor discovery case at a non-crisis moment was not found.

#### Channel: eBay Certified Provider directory
- Buyer presence: yes, in principle.
- Entrant access: eBay operates a Certified Provider program (developer.ebay.com, primary) with two tiers. Secondary sources (devx.com, webopedia.com) describe qualification as requiring "extensive experience with eBay," passing "a strict certification exam," and providing "a number of proven customer references."
- Discovery mechanism: n/a — gated before discovery is possible.
- Recent newcomer evidence: none possible by design.
- Structural restrictions: **explicitly requires pre-existing traction and customer references** — fails Path B criterion (4) outright.
- Source/date: developer.ebay.com/programs/certifiedprovider (primary), accessed 2026-09-22.
- Evidence strength: **Not a viable channel for a new entrant — confirmed structurally gated.**

#### Channel: Shopify App Store
- Buyer presence: weak fit — the buyer here is eBay/Etsy sellers, not Shopify merchants; relevance rests on one reported mention (andy.johns_4 finding LitCommerce there).
- Entrant access: self-serve listing open to any developer.
- Discovery mechanism: Shopify's own community-forum staff answer (community.shopify.com, primary/semi-official) states ranking depends on "app description, total installs, total uninstalls, use of Billing APIs, search terms, and reviews" — confirmed install/review-weighted, consistent with Candidate 2's finding on the same platform.
- Recent newcomer evidence: the single LitCommerce mention is anecdotal, not independently reverified, and does not establish a generalizable pattern.
- Structural restrictions: fails Path B criterion (4) per Shopify's own documentation.
- Source/date: community.shopify.com (primary/semi-official), accessed 2026-09-22.
- Evidence strength: **Weak/not evidence as a repeatable channel for this buyer — single unverified anecdote, structurally review-weighted.**

#### Channel: Etsy seller forums/communities
- Buyer presence: yes, for Etsy sellers.
- Entrant access: Etsy's Community Policy (etsy.com, primary) prohibits "unsolicited advertising or promotions." Etsy also made its Community Forum **private** and restricted API access (secondary, valueaddedresource.net, referencing a 2024/2025 platform change), reducing external visibility of discovery threads.
- Discovery mechanism: unconfirmed for organic vendor discovery.
- Recent newcomer evidence: none found — only generic "third-party access restricted" commentary.
- Source/date: etsy.com/legal/community (primary); valueaddedresource.net (secondary), accessed 2026-09-22.
- Evidence strength: **Weak / no evidence found.**

#### Channel: Comparison/review sites and listicle content
- Buyer presence: plausible but unconfirmed as a discovery source specifically.
- Entrant access: numerous "best cross-listing app 2026" roundups exist (Vendoo, CLOSO, Crosslist, Threecolts, Resylr, ResaleOS), but these are vendor-adjacent/SEO content, including some competitor-authored "best of" lists featuring themselves.
- Discovery mechanism: unconfirmed as an actual buyer-side discovery event.
- Recent newcomer evidence: none confirmed.
- Evidence strength: **Weak — the "there's content/listicles" pattern the standard explicitly excludes; not stacked with other channels.**

### Strongest verified distribution path
The eBay Community peer-forum Q&A mechanism is the strongest finding across all three candidates in this gate: it is demonstrably a standing, repeatable community behavior (confirmed via activity independent of the InkFrog shutdown), open to organic peer recommendation (though closed to vendor self-promotion), and it did produce two named small-vendor discovery instances within the last 12 months. It falls short of a clean Path A/Path B confirmation only because those two specific instances are both tied to the shutdown event, and no non-event instance of a small vendor being surfaced this way was found.

### Distribution assessment
This candidate has the most differentiated and specific positive evidence of the three, but the task's own explicit warning — not to treat a shutdown-driven migration spike as proof of a repeatable channel — is directly on point here and was not fully answered: the *mechanism* (peer Q&A) is confirmed repeatable, but a *small-vendor discovery instance independent of the crisis* is not yet confirmed. eBay's Certified Provider directory is confirmed structurally closed to new entrants. Shopify App Store is a weak fit for this specific buyer and independently confirmed review-weighted. Etsy forums returned no usable evidence.

### Gate result
**BLOCKED / INCONCLUSIVE**

### Remaining unknowns
Whether the eBay Community peer-Q&A mechanism has ever surfaced a genuinely small/new vendor (not InkFrog, not an established multi-year tool) outside the shutdown-driven thread specifically — this is the single fact that would resolve this candidate to PASS. Whether eBay operates any other organic discovery surface beyond the Certified Provider directory and open community forums. Whether Etsy's forum-privacy change has a workaround relevant to vendor discovery.

## Cross-Candidate Observations

*Methodological patterns only — no opportunity judgment or ranking.*

- **Marketplace/directory listing is consistently not equivalent to newcomer discoverability across all three candidates.** Every app-store-style channel investigated (Shopify App Store for Candidates 2 and 3, QuickBooks App Store, Xero App Marketplace, Amazon Selling Partner Appstore, Applied Epic's certification program, eBay's Certified Provider directory) either explicitly documents an install/review-weighted ranking mechanism, a paid listing tier, or a pre-existing-traction/reference requirement. In every case investigated, the platform's own documentation was the source of this finding, not speculation.
- **Review-based/installs-based discovery structurally favors incumbents, and this was independently confirmed on two separate platforms (Shopify, in both Candidates 2 and 3) using the platform's own stated ranking factors** — not inferred from competitor review counts, which the gate explicitly excludes as evidence.
- **A concentrated migration event (InkFrog's shutdown, Candidate 3) produced real discovery instances for small vendors, but did not by itself establish a repeatable channel** — the underlying peer-Q&A mechanism was independently confirmed to recur outside the event, which is meaningfully more evidence than the event alone would provide, but the specific small-vendor-discovery outcome remains event-linked in the evidence gathered. This is the clearest illustration across all three candidates of why the task's distinction between "temporary migration opportunity" and "repeatable distribution channel" matters in practice, not just in principle.
- **Certification/partner programs that appear open on paper can still carry an informal traction bar visible only in who actually gets accepted** — Applied Epic's Vendor Certification Program (Candidate 1) states no explicit customer-count requirement, but its entire first cohort consisted of already-funded, already-customered insurtechs. Written eligibility rules and observed outcomes should be checked separately; they did not agree in this case.
- **Paid-to-play channels (conference exhibit halls, tiered marketplace listing fees) were found in two of three candidates (Applied Net booths for Candidate 1; Xero's Plus-tier listing requirement for Candidate 2) and were excluded from consideration as distribution evidence**, since they are structurally incompatible with the project's no-cold-outreach-cost constraint, not because they don't work as channels in general.
