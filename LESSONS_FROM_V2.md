# Lessons From `nishe_scout`

This is a compressed record of the previous experiment. It is deliberately short.

## What happened

The previous project spent substantial effort researching Atlassian Marketplace opportunities through:

`marketplace → keywords → competitors → pain → wedge`

Several plausible wedges were investigated and then killed because:

- native functionality already covered the proposed wedge;
- existing Marketplace apps already covered the supposedly narrow gap;
- support/integration burden was high;
- buyer/payment intent was weak or indirect;
- low install/review counts did not prove acquisition opportunity.

## Important methodological lesson

A real problem is not automatically a viable micro-SaaS opportunity.

We need all of these to connect:

`real pain + buyer + money + dissatisfaction + narrow solution + discoverability`

Missing the distribution step was a major weakness.

## Marketplace lesson

A marketplace can provide discovery, but marketplace presence alone does not prove that a new unknown vendor can obtain organic installs.

The previous research also found a counterexample to the stronger claim that first-time single-app vendors cannot appear in marketplace discovery. That means such entry is possible, but it was not sufficiently common or well evidenced to justify assuming marketplace cold-start distribution works.

Therefore:

- do not declare marketplace entry impossible;
- do not assume marketplace cold-start distribution works;
- test the actual acquisition path.

## Research-method lesson

Client-side rendered marketplace search required browser-based verification. Static fetching produced misleading identical results for different queries.

General rule:

> Verify that the research method actually observes the data the user sees.

## Strategic lesson

Do not allow an unresolved candidate to justify another large research phase.

Once a candidate has survived problem/buyer/payment/distribution gates, move to cheap validation.

Once a candidate repeatedly fails those gates, stop.

## What we do NOT carry forward

We do not carry forward:

- the old Atlassian-first strategy;
- endless keyword sweeps;
- arbitrary scoring systems;
- competitor counts as standalone evidence;
- the assumption that the next phase will reveal the answer;
- "research first, build later" as an unlimited process.

`nishe_scout` remains an archive and evidence base, not the template for this project.
